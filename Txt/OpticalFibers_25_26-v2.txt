? 2 main sources of nonlinear phenomena:
- Non-linear Phase Modulation: Kerr Effect (included in Shrödinger's equation)
• SPM, XPM, FWM
- Stimulated light scattering (not included in Shrödinger's equation)
• Other effects arise from delayed and inelastic material responses and are not captured by the instantaneous Kerr nonlinearity used to derive the NLSE used in this chapter
• Scattering of a photon to a lower energy photon: energy difference appears in the form of a phonon (collective excitation of atoms or molecules)
• Stimulated Raman Scattering (SRS): optical phonons
• Stimulated Brillouin Scattering (SBS): acoustic phonons
• Overall effect: loss of power at the incident frequency, gain on other frequencies



? SRS
- Pump wave is scattered by the silica molecules
- Some pump photons give up their energy to lower-energy photons and the remaining energy is absorbed by silica molecules
- Silica molecules vibrate
- Occurs in all directions (contrary to SBS)
- Scattered wave is shifted in frequency
- Beating between pump and scattered wave in both directions continues to stimulate molecular oscillations: positive feedback
- Can shift the scattered wavelength by up to dozens of THz (depends on the decay time of the vibrating molecules)
- Threshold incident power

? SBS
- Electrostriction: tendency of materials to become compressed in the presence of an electric field
- An incident optical pulsing wave (CW, pump) generates an
acoustic wave at the opposite direction from the channel
- Results in the change of the optical pulse frequency
- The beating between the scattered wave and the pump increases the acoustic wave amplitude: positive feedback
- All power of the pump can be transferred to the scattered wave
- The scattered light is shifted by about 10 GHz
- Threshold launched power should not be exceeded
- The same effect is expected for a high bit-rate modulated signal, but with a higher threshold power
